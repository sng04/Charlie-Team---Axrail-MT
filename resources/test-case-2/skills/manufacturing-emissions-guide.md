# Manufacturing Sector Emissions Guide

## Common Emission Sources in Manufacturing

### Stationary Combustion
Most manufacturing facilities burn natural gas for process heat, steam generation, and space heating. Emission factor: approximately 53.06 kg CO2 per MMBtu of natural gas.

For facilities with on-site diesel generators (backup power), emission factor: 73.96 kg CO2 per MMBtu.

### Industrial Processes
Some manufacturing processes release CO2 directly through chemical reactions, not combustion:
- **Cement**: Calcination of limestone (CaCO3 → CaO + CO2) accounts for ~60% of cement industry emissions
- **Steel**: Basic oxygen furnace steelmaking using coal/coke as a reductant
- **Glass**: Decomposition of soda ash and limestone in glass melting
- **Chemicals**: Various catalytic and thermal cracking processes

### Refrigerants
Industrial refrigeration and HVAC systems use hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) with high global warming potential (GWP). Common refrigerants:
- R-134a: GWP 1,430
- R-404A: GWP 3,922
- R-410A: GWP 2,088

Even small leaks are significant. A facility losing 10 kg of R-404A per year releases the equivalent of 39 tonnes of CO2.

## Data Collection Tips for Manufacturing Clients

1. **Start with utility bills**: Natural gas and electricity invoices for the past 24 months. These cover 70-80% of Scope 1 and 2 emissions.
2. **Fleet fuel cards**: If the company operates delivery trucks or forklifts, fuel card statements provide accurate consumption data.
3. **Production logs**: Units produced per month. Needed to calculate emission intensity (emissions per unit of output).
4. **Refrigerant purchase records**: HVAC maintenance logs showing refrigerant top-ups. Each top-up represents a leak.
5. **Waste manifests**: Waste hauler reports showing tonnes sent to landfill vs. recycling. Needed for Scope 3 Category 5.
