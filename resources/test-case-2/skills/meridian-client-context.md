# Meridian Manufacturing — Client Context

## Company Profile

- **Industry**: Automotive parts manufacturing (Tier 1 supplier)
- **Products**: Brake assemblies, suspension components
- **Facilities**: 3 plants (2 in Ohio, 1 in Tennessee)
- **Employees**: ~1,200
- **Annual Revenue**: $340M
- **Key Customers**: Ford, Stellantis, General Motors

## Known Environmental Profile

Meridian's plants primarily use:
- Natural gas for heat treatment furnaces and paint curing ovens
- Electricity for CNC machining, hydraulic presses, and facility HVAC
- Diesel for on-site forklifts and a small delivery fleet (~15 trucks)
- R-410A refrigerant in plant HVAC systems

Estimated annual energy consumption (from preliminary utility data shared during pre-sales):
- Natural gas: ~180,000 MMBtu/year across all three plants
- Electricity: ~42,000 MWh/year across all three plants
- Diesel fuel: ~120,000 gallons/year (fleet)

## Preliminary Emission Estimate

Based on industry benchmarks for automotive parts manufacturing:
- Scope 1 (direct combustion + fleet): ~12,000 - 15,000 tCO2e/year
- Scope 2 (purchased electricity): ~18,000 - 22,000 tCO2e/year (Ohio and Tennessee grids are coal-heavy)
- Scope 3 (Categories 1, 4, 5): ~40,000 - 60,000 tCO2e/year (dominated by purchased steel)

Total estimated footprint: 70,000 - 97,000 tCO2e/year

## Key Risks and Opportunities

**Risks:**
- High grid emission factor in Ohio (AEP Ohio territory ~0.55 tCO2/MWh) makes Scope 2 substantial
- Steel procurement is the largest Scope 3 driver — BOF steel has ~2x the carbon intensity of EAF steel
- Refrigerant data may be incomplete — maintenance records are fragmented across plants

**Opportunities:**
- Switching to EAF steel suppliers could reduce Scope 3 by 15-25%
- Renewable energy procurement (PPA or green tariff) could reduce Scope 2 by 40-60%
- LED lighting and VFD upgrades on HVAC could reduce electricity consumption by 8-12%
